Grapevine Reader Privacy Policy

Last updated: 22 October 2026

Grapevine Reader ("Grapevine") is a native Windows feed reader. It has no account, no sign-up and no telemetry, and everything it stores stays on your PC. Because it is an internet reader it does talk to servers: this policy says exactly which, when, and what they can see. Every statement below describes the code as shipped; where a behaviour is a setting, the default is named.

Summary

  • No account, no tracking, no telemetry. Nothing about your use of the app is collected or sent to the developer or to anyone else.
  • Your data stays on your device, in one folder you can open, back up and delete.
  • Network requests go to the sources you chose and the sites their articles reference. The few other destinations - a search engine and a translator you can invoke from selected text, an optional icon lookup, and Microsoft's own services inside the built-in browser - are listed below.

Data stored on your device

Everything lives under %LOCALAPPDATA%\MathewNewman\Grapevine, packaged and unpackaged builds alike. Settings > Storage > Open data folder opens it and Back up... copies the library out of it. Uninstalling the app and deleting the folder removes everything.

None of it is uploaded anywhere.

Network connections the app makes

Feeds you subscribe to. Fetched when you refresh, on startup (default on), every 30 minutes while the app runs, and - packaged builds only - by a Windows background task every 30 minutes while the app is closed. All of this is under Settings > Startup & syncing. Each request identifies the app by its name and version and carries only that, compression negotiation, and the conditional validators the feed itself sent last time. No cookies are ever stored or sent, no referrer, no credentials. A feed that redirects off the web or from https to http is refused.

Adding a site address. When what you type is a page rather than a feed, the app fetches that page, follows the feed it declares, or otherwise probes /feed, /feed.xml, /index.xml and /rss.xml on that same site. An http:// feed is probed once for an https:// twin and upgraded when the same feed answers.

Article pages. With Extract full articles on (the default), after each refresh the app fetches the pages behind new unread articles whose feed carries only a summary, so they read instantly and offline. Those sites therefore see a visit before you open the article. Turn it off in Settings and the page is fetched only when you open such an article.

Images in articles. By default (Always) images load from the sites that host them, which shows those sites your address. Settings > Privacy can restrict this to images on the article's own site (Same site) or to a Load-image tap (Manual). Cached images always show. Images go through the app's own cookie-free client and are stored in the image cache; one the cache declines - larger than 5 MB, or not served as an image - is shown by the Windows image stack directly, which makes its own connection to that host under Windows' settings rather than the app's.

Feed icons. Taken from the feed's own site. An optional fallback (off by default) sends only a site's hostname to google.com when the site offers no icon.

The built-in website view. Grapevine embeds Microsoft Edge WebView2 to show a page as a browser would. It opens a page when you choose View website; when an article could not be extracted and Show the website when an article can't be extracted is on (off by default from this version); and for feeds the app has learned never carry readable text, where it opens the page without waiting. Inside it, pages behave as in a browser: scripts run unless you switch them off (Settings > Privacy > JavaScript, on by default), and the page's cookies and site data are kept under WebView2\ until you clear them (Settings > Privacy > Clear website data, or the clear-on-exit switch). Form autofill and password saving are off, so nothing you type into a page is remembered by the app. The app strips the referrer on cross-site requests, contains pop-ups, cancels downloads and denies permission prompts. Microsoft's SmartScreen check is left at the WebView2 runtime's default, so addresses of pages opened in the website view may be sent to Microsoft under Microsoft's privacy terms; the runtime's own update and component traffic to Microsoft is outside the app's control. Unpacked extensions you place under Extensions\ are loaded into the website view with whatever access their own manifests claim.

Selected text. From text selected in the reader, Search opens duckduckgo.com and Translate opens bing.com/translator in your default browser with that text, and only when you choose them.

Links you open. Common tracking parameters (utm_*, gclid, fbclid and similar) are removed from a link before it is handed to your browser.

Proxies. If your network requires an authenticating proxy, the app presents your Windows sign-in to that proxy, as Edge does; nothing else ever receives it.

Sharing into the app (the share sheet, feed: links, .opml files) only receives; it sends nothing.

Read aloud uses the voices installed on your PC. Notifications and the taskbar badge are local.

Diagnostics kept on your device

crash.log gains one line per launch describing the machine - app version and packaging, Windows App SDK and Windows build, CPU architecture, display scale and work area, WebView2 runtime version, backdrop support, power mode, culture and clock, and a few display-preference flags - plus a line for any fault the app records. A fault line can name the article or page the fault happened on, so the file can contain addresses you have read. background-sync.log records each background run's outcome. Neither is ever sent; if you attach one to a bug report, read it first and remove anything you would rather not share.

Third parties

The app has no advertising, analytics or social SDKs. What a feed server, an article's site, an image host, the search engine or the translator learns from a request is governed by their own privacy policies. The website view runs on the Microsoft Edge WebView2 runtime under Microsoft's terms.

Children

Grapevine is a general-audience reading tool and is not directed at children.
